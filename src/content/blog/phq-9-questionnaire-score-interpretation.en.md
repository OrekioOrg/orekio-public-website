The PHQ-9 (*Patient Health Questionnaire*, 9-item version) is one of the most widely used self-report questionnaires for exploring depressive symptoms. It is short, free to use, and available in many validated languages. In a follow-up, it mainly serves as a point of comparison from one appointment to the next.

## What it measures

The 9 items map onto the symptom criteria for a depressive episode: mood, loss of interest, sleep, energy, appetite, concentration, guilt, slowing or agitation, and thoughts of death or self-harm. For each one, the patient indicates the frequency over the **past two weeks**:

- 0: not at all
- 1: several days
- 2: more than half the days
- 3: nearly every day

The total score ranges from 0 to 27.

## What the thresholds say

The commonly cited bands are: 5 (mild), 10 (moderate), 15 (moderately severe), 20 (severe). A score of 10 or above is often used as an attention threshold for major depressive disorder.

These numbers remain a **monitoring marker**: diagnosis stays a clinical act, made in interview. The PHQ-9 complements that interview, which keeps the final word. Item 9, on thoughts of death or self-harm, calls for a direct assessment by the practitioner whatever the answer.

## What Orekio does with it, and what stays with the practitioner

In Orekio, the PHQ-9 is a module the practitioner unlocks when they judge it useful. The patient fills it in from their phone, in a version faithful to the validated questionnaire. At the next appointment, the practitioner finds the total score, the item-by-item detail, and the history of administrations.

Orekio **displays** this result, and stops there. The label ("moderate depression"), the comparison to a norm and the alert, including on item 9, belong to the practitioner. The clinical reading, the course of action and the assessment of suicide risk are entirely the practitioner's responsibility.

In practice, the PHQ-9 in Orekio makes a data point available, and vigilance remains whole: it is up to the practitioner to organise the framework around it (frequency of administration, interview, [safety plan](/en/blog/plan-de-securite-comment-le-construire) where appropriate).

## In practice

One administration before the first appointment gives a baseline. Repeated every two to four weeks, the PHQ-9 lets you track change under the effect of a treatment or psychotherapy, with a numerical support the patient and practitioner look at together. The GAD-7 (anxiety) and the sleep diary are often used alongside it.
