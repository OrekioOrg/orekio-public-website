The PHQ-9 (*Patient Health Questionnaire*, 9-item version) is one of the most widely used self-report questionnaires for exploring depressive symptoms. It is short, free of rights, and available in many translations. From one appointment to the next, it mainly serves as a point of comparison.

## What it measures

The 9 items map onto the symptom criteria for a depressive episode: mood, loss of interest, sleep, energy, appetite, concentration, guilt, slowing or agitation, and thoughts of death or self-harm. For each one, the patient indicates the frequency over the past two weeks:

- 0: not at all
- 1: several days
- 2: more than half the days
- 3: nearly every day

The total score ranges from 0 to 27.

## What the thresholds say

The bands from the validation study (Kroenke, Spitzer and Williams, 2001) are: 5 (mild), 10 (moderate), 15 (moderately severe), 20 (severe). A score of 10 or more is often used as the attention threshold for a major depressive episode: in that study, it corresponded to a sensitivity and a specificity of 88%.

These figures remain a marker: diagnosis stays a clinical act, made in interview. The PHQ-9 complements that interview, which keeps the final word. Any answer other than 0 to item 9, on thoughts of death or self-harm, calls for a direct assessment of suicide risk in interview.

## In practice

A first administration, at the start of care, gives a baseline. Many practitioners have it repeated every two to four weeks, during a treatment or a psychotherapy, as a numerical support the patient and you look at together. It is you who set the rhythm, in consultation; Orekio simply displays the administrations one below the other. The GAD-7 and the [sleep diary](/en/blog/agenda-du-sommeil-entre-deux-rendez-vous) are often used alongside.
