The PHQ-9 (*Patient Health Questionnaire*, 9-item version) is one of the most widely used self-report questionnaires for exploring depressive symptoms. It is short, free to use, and available in many validated languages. In a follow-up, it mainly serves as a point of comparison from one appointment to the next.

## What it measures

The 9 items map onto the symptom criteria for a depressive episode: mood, loss of interest, sleep, energy, appetite, concentration, guilt, slowing or agitation, and thoughts of death or self-harm. For each one, the patient indicates the frequency over the **past two weeks**:

- 0 — not at all
- 1 — several days
- 2 — more than half the days
- 3 — nearly every day

The total score ranges from 0 to 27.

## What the thresholds say

The commonly cited bands are: 5 (mild), 10 (moderate), 15 (moderately severe), 20 (severe). A score of 10 or above is often used as an attention threshold for major depressive disorder.

These numbers are **not a diagnosis**. The PHQ-9 is a screening and monitoring tool: it complements the clinical interview, it does not replace it. Item 9, on thoughts of death or self-harm, calls for a direct assessment by the practitioner whatever the answer.

## What Orekio does with it — and doesn't

In Orekio, the PHQ-9 is a module the practitioner unlocks when they judge it useful. The patient fills it in from their phone, in a version faithful to the validated questionnaire. At the next appointment, the practitioner finds the total score, the item-by-item detail, and the history of administrations.

Orekio **does not interpret** this result. The app shows no label ("moderate depression"), does not compare the score to a norm, and **triggers no alert** — including on item 9. This is a deliberate choice: Orekio holds the status of a digital care journal, outside the scope of Regulation (EU) 2017/745 on medical devices (see the [About](/en/a-propos) page). The clinical reading, the course of action and the assessment of suicide risk are entirely the practitioner's responsibility.

In practice, this means the PHQ-9 in Orekio does not substitute for any vigilance: it makes a data point available, and it is up to the practitioner to organise the framework around it (frequency of administration, interview, [safety plan](/en/blog/plan-de-securite-comment-le-construire) where appropriate).

## In practice

One administration before the first appointment gives a baseline. Repeated every two to four weeks, the PHQ-9 lets you track change under the effect of a treatment or psychotherapy, with a numerical support the patient and practitioner look at together. The [GAD-7](/en/fonctionnalites) (anxiety) and sleep-diary modules are often used alongside it.
