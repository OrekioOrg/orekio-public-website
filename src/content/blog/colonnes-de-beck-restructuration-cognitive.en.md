Beck's columns, also called a *thought record*, are one of the central tools of cognitive behavioral therapy. The principle: when a strong emotion arises, you note the situation and the thought that came with it, then examine that thought rather than treating it as a fact.

## The formats

The short format has three columns:

1. **Situation**: where, when, with whom, what was happening;
2. **Automatic thought**: what went through the mind, written as it was;
3. **Emotion**: named, and if possible rated for intensity (0 to 100).

The extended format adds: the **evidence for** and **against** the thought, a more balanced **alternative thought**, and a re-rating of the emotion afterwards. Greenberger and Padesky's seven-column thought record (*Mind Over Mood*) is one of the most widely used versions.

## What it's for

Writing slows down reasoning and makes recurring **cognitive distortions** visible: catastrophizing, mind-reading, all-or-nothing, overgeneralization. With repetition, the patient spots their patterns faster and more easily reaches an alternative reading, one tuned to the facts rather than an optimistic one.

The record is filled in as close as possible to the moment the emotion occurred; filled in several days later, it loses precision. It is a support for reflection, reviewed and worked on in the appointment: what matters is the process, more than a score or a right answer.

## What Orekio does with it, and what stays with the practitioner

In Orekio, Beck's columns are a module the practitioner unlocks, usually after some initial work on emotions (for example with the [wheel of emotions](/en/blog/roue-des-emotions-en-therapie)). The patient fills in their records from their phone, between appointments. The practitioner then finds them in full.

Orekio **displays** these records as the patient wrote them, column by column, with the date. Spotting distortions, scoring and clinical comment belong to the practitioner. Consistent with its status as a digital care journal, outside the scope of medical devices (see [About](/en/a-propos)): the clinical analysis happens in the appointment.

## In practice

Two to four records a week is often enough at first. The goal is regularity rather than volume, and above all the shared review in the appointment, where practitioner and patient examine one or two situations in detail together. The [PHQ-9](/en/blog/phq-9-questionnaire-score-interpretation) and GAD-7 scales are sometimes used to track change alongside it.
