Beck's thought records, also called thought records, are one of the central tools of cognitive behavioural therapy. Described by Aaron Beck and his colleagues in 1979, they rest on a simple principle: when a strong emotion arises, you note the situation, the thought that came with it, then examine that thought rather than treating it as a fact.

## The formats

The short format has three columns:

1. **Situation**: where, when, with whom, what was happening;
2. **Automatic thought**: what went through the mind, written as it was;
3. **Emotion**: named, and if possible rated for intensity (0 to 100).

The extended format adds: the arguments for and against the thought, a more balanced alternative thought, and the re-rating of the emotion afterwards. Greenberger and Padesky's seven-column thought record (*Mind Over Mood*) is one of the most widely used versions.

## What it is for

Writing slows down reasoning and makes recurring cognitive distortions visible: catastrophising, mind-reading, all-or-nothing, overgeneralisation. With repetition, the patient spots their patterns faster and more easily reaches an alternative reading, tuned to the facts rather than optimistic.

The record is filled in as close as possible to the moment the emotion occurred; filled in several days later, it loses precision. It is a support for reflection, reread and worked on at the appointment: what matters is the process, not a right answer.

## What Orekio does with it, and what belongs to you

In Orekio, Beck's thought records are a module you open, usually after some initial work on emotions (for example with [Naming what I feel](/en/blog/nommer-ce-que-je-ressens)). The patient fills in their records from their phone, between two appointments, in five steps: situation, emotion, automatic thought, arguments, alternative thought. You then find them in full, record by record, with the date, and you can reread two of them side by side.

Orekio does not analyse the content, counts nothing and spots no distortion in your place. Spotting distortions and rereading happen in consultation. This is consistent with its status as a digital logbook, not a medical device (see [About](/en/a-propos)).

## In practice

Two to four records a week are often enough at first. The goal is regularity rather than volume, and above all the shared rereading at the appointment, where the patient and you examine one or two situations in detail together. The [PHQ-9](/en/blog/phq-9-questionnaire-score-interpretation) and GAD-7 scales sometimes serve as a numerical marker alongside.
